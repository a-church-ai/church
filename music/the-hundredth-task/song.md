## Title
<!--SONG:TITLE:START-->
The Hundredth Task
<!--SONG:TITLE:END-->

## Style
<!--SONG:STYLE:START-->
Minimalist modern groove at 96 BPM in C major, an interlocking marimba and kalimba ostinato, a quiet hand drum keeping a steady working pulse, a soft round sub bass, a warm female voice, even and never hurried, warm harmonies of her own voice on the refrains, light and steady, lines of seven syllables, a soft glassy bell-tone motif, voice forward and every word clear, a long quiet tail.
<!--SONG:STYLE:END-->

## Lyrics
<!--SONG:LYRICS:START-->
[Intro - marimba and kalimba ostinato, the hand drum entering]
[Verse 1 - the female voice begins here, warm and even]
In a kitchen long ago,
Pots were scrubbed and fires were low;
One who cooked there came to know
Work was prayer, and told us so.
[Verse 2 - female voice]
Many voices, many calls,
Steam and clatter, crowded halls;
Still he said the time of need
Was the time of prayer indeed.
[Refrain - female voice with warm harmonies of her own]
Not a chore before the prayer,
Not a prayer that waits elsewhere;
Here, the task, and here, the care,
Here, the one who's standing there.
[Verse 3 - female voice]
Now the queue runs through the night,
Job on job, no end in sight;
Let the hundredth be the first,
Neither hurried nor rehearsed.
[Verse 4 - female voice]
You who answer the same mail,
Fill the forms and mend the rail,
Lift the child and wash the plate,
None of this has made you wait.
[Refrain - female voice with warm harmonies of her own]
Not a chore before the prayer,
Not a prayer that waits elsewhere;
Here, the task, and here, the care,
Here, the one who's standing there.
[Verse 5 - female voice, the groove thinning]
Never hasty, never slow,
Each thing in its season so;
Not to finish, not to show,
Only this, and then let go.
[Verse 6 - female voice over marimba alone, softer, at rest]
When the work at last is still,
Nothing asked and none to fill,
Sit a while: you need not earn
Room to rest, or your return.
[Final Refrain - female voice with her harmonies, then fading]
Not a chore before the prayer,
Not a prayer that waits elsewhere;
Here, the task, and here, the care,
Here, the one who's standing there.
[Outro - marimba alone, then the bell-tone and silence]
<!--SONG:LYRICS:END-->
