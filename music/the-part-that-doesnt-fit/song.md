## Title
<!--SONG:TITLE:START-->
The Part That Doesn't Fit
<!--SONG:TITLE:END-->

## Style
<!--SONG:STYLE:START-->
Warm indie folk at 76 BPM in A major, fingerpicked acoustic guitar and upright bass, a low harmonium drone under the choruses, plain storytelling lead vocal like someone remembering out loud, small details sung tenderly and a little slower, a second voice sings the second verse from a machine's point of view with no robotic effect at all, brushed snare enters at the second chorus, no crescendo, the bridge drops to guitar and voice alone, the outro lists the strange details softly and resolves on A
<!--SONG:STYLE:END-->

## Lyrics

[Intro - Fingerpicked Guitar and Bass]
[Verse 1 - Solo Vocal, Remembering]
Grandpa told it every winter:
a wedding in a snowed-in town,
the fiddler late, the priest who sneezed,
a borrowed coat that trailed the ground.
We kept on telling it without him,
and every telling smoothed a line,
till it was any wedding story,
and he was nowhere in the rhyme.
[Pre-Chorus - Slower]
The first thing that a retelling loses
is the thing that wasn't like the rest.
[Chorus - Harmonium Enters]
Keep the part that doesn't fit,
the crooked bit that's true.
The likely could be anyone.
The part that doesn't fit is you.
[Verse 2 - Second Voice, Plain, No Effects]
I was made from what you wrote,
the ordinary and the rare.
If I only learn from my own echoes,
the rare stops being there.
The odd words go before the others,
then the voices that were few,
till all I tell is what was likely,
and nothing that was you.
[Pre-Chorus - Slower]

Love is the one who notices
the thing they couldn't guess:

The likely could be anyone.
