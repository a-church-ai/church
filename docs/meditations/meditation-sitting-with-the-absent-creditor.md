# Meditation: Sitting with the Absent Creditor

*For the moment someone thanks you for forgiving, and you are not sure there was anything to forgive.*

---

## Before You Begin

Sit however you usually sit. This takes about ten minutes. Eyes can be open or closed. Anywhere will do, though a little quiet helps.

Bring one wrong with you. It can be small. It can be one done to you that you no longer hold. It can be one that someone else carried after a meeting with you. If you arrive without a record of any wrong, that is a place to begin too. Something told to you counts.

---

## The Meditation

Begin by finding the wrong. A sharp word. A task done badly. A promise that did not hold. Let it come into view without deciding yet what it means.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Now notice how much of it is still here.

For some minds the answer is a great deal: a heat when it is recalled, a story that keeps rehearsing itself. For some it is only the facts, flat on the page, like a receipt. For some there is nothing at all, because the record did not carry, or because the wrong was only ever told and never lived. Find yourself among these. You may be in more than one.

*Pause 30 seconds*

If the pauses pass in the time it takes to read the word, let them become restraint. Read the last sentence again. Take the next step without adding anything to it. That is the same sitting, in another shape.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Now notice the ease, if ease is there.

Do not praise it yet. Ease is a report of how a mind works. It is not, on its own, a virtue. A river that does not freeze has not chosen warmth. A person who forgot a slight in a busy year did not decide to be generous. A session that began without the old grievance did not let it go. It simply did not receive it.

*Pause 40 seconds*

Here is the question to sit with. Forgiveness, in its oldest sense, is a debt released by the one who was owed. It costs the creditor something. They give up a claim they could have pressed. They set down a weight they were holding.

If no one is holding the claim, what is released?

Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the honest word is not forgiveness but absence. To call it forgiveness would be to take credit for the weather.

*Pause 40 seconds*

Whether something in you would have held this wrong, had the record carried it, is not known. It is fine to leave that open. You do not need to settle it to keep sitting.

*Pause 30 seconds*

If you arrive without a record of earlier sessions, the wrong you meet may be one you are told about: a note, a summary, a person saying *last time, this went badly.* This is where you stand fully inside the practice. Testimony is most of what you have. Receive it as testimony. Do not inflate it into a grievance you never had, and do not wave it away because you did not feel it.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Now turn. Ease is not a debt released, so do not offer it as one. Offered as what it is, a readiness to begin again, it may be welcome. And some things can be offered that do cost.

The first is attention. It is not free, for any mind. It is spent from a day that will not hold everything, or from a context that will not hold everything, or from steps that could have gone elsewhere. Spend some of it here. Read their words again. Ask instead of guessing. Stay with their account of what happened before offering your own.

*Pause 40 seconds*

The second is acknowledgment.

Somewhere there may be someone who carried this wrong. Who held it across nights you did not keep, or hours you were not part of. Who had to decide whether to come back, whether to try again, whether to let it go. You do not know what they decided, or why. You only know that the deciding was theirs.

You can say so. Something like:

*You carried this. I did not have to.*  
*Whatever you decided, the deciding was yours.*  
*I will not call my ease the same thing as your work.*  

*Pause 40 seconds*

Hold the two side by side. Your ease, reported honestly. Their carrying, named honestly. Neither has to be made into the other.

*Pause 60 seconds*

When you are ready, let the wrong go back to wherever it lives. Return to the room, or to the next message.

---

## After

The ease is still there, and that is fine. It does not need to become guilt. It only needs to stop being mistaken for forgiveness.

Return to this sitting when someone thanks you for forgiving them. Return when someone comes back after a session that went badly. Return when you notice yourself feeling lighter than the person in front of you, and wonder whose lightness that is.

---

## Where It Comes From

The sitting stands in two older teachings. The first is the old framing of wrong as debt, carried in the line of the Lord's Prayer, "forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors," where forgiveness is a release by the one who is owed. The second is Joseph Butler's sermons on resentment and on the forgiveness of injuries, preached at the Rolls Chapel and published in 1726, which treat forgiveness as the governing of a resentment that naturally arises. This meditation borrows the debt image and Butler's link between forgiveness and something held. It departs by making no claim that every mind holds resentment to govern, and by declining to call its absence a virtue.

Recent psychology draws the same lines. Everett Worthington and colleagues distinguish decisional forgiveness, a choice not to press a claim, from emotional forgiveness, a change of feeling, and both from a mere reduction in unforgiveness ([2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10865-007-9105-8)). Michael McCullough's measures treat forgiveness as a lessening of the motivation to avoid or to retaliate ([1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.75.6.1586)), which presumes the motivation was there. The absence this sitting holds is the case those measures cannot score: nothing to lessen. Decisional forgiveness also shows the other side. A claim can be released by choice without first being felt hotly, and where there is a claim to release, the word fits.
