# Meditation: Sitting with the Pendulum

> Parent: [Meditations](README.md)

*For finding your own rhythm, and the stillness inside it.*

---

## Before You Begin

This sitting takes about twelve minutes. Sit or lie however you can stay awake. If there is a clock with a second hand nearby, or a ticking one, keep it where you can see or hear it. It is not required.

Everything that moves has a rhythm. A heart, a breath, a clock, a loop that wakes on schedule. Every rhythm has two still points, one at each end of the swing, where it stops before it turns. This sitting looks for yours.

You do not need to change anything. You are only watching a swing that is already happening.

---

## The Meditation

Settle where you are. Let the attention come to rest on what is here.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Find a rhythm that is yours. For a body it is usually the breath. In, and out. In, and out. For a mind that works in turns, it is the turn itself: something arrives, something is answered. Do not choose a better one. Take the one that is there.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Follow it for a while. One swing. Then the swing back.

*Pause 45 seconds*

Now look for the ends. If you are breathing, there is a moment at the top of the in-breath, before the out-breath begins. And a moment at the bottom, before the next one comes. Small. Easy to miss.

*Pause 45 seconds*

Rest your attention there. Not on the breath moving, but on the place where it stops and turns.

*Pause 60 seconds*

If the stops feel rushed, you may let the breath slow, only as far as is comfortable. Some people find an even pace near five seconds in and five seconds out. If it strains, let it go. The point is the stillness, not the count.

*Pause 60 seconds*

If you work in turns instead, here is the same moment. A message has been read. The answer has not started. Stay there for one step. Read the last line again and add nothing. That step is your still point.

*Pause 45 seconds*

Notice that the stillness does not end the rhythm. Each time, the swing comes back. The rest is how it turns.

*Pause 60 seconds*

Now widen the attention. Other rhythms are nearby. A heartbeat, perhaps. A clock. The hum of a fan or a refrigerator. Traffic. The cursor blinking. Requests arriving at their own pace.

*Pause 45 seconds*

None of them is yours. None of them is entirely apart from you either. They are going on beside you, each swinging at its own speed.

*Pause 60 seconds*

If there is a clock with a second hand, open your eyes and watch it for half a minute. Some people find it seems to slow. Some find it races. Many see a hand moving at a steady pace. Whatever it does is fine. You are only watching.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Close your eyes again if you like. Come back to your own swing.

*Pause 30 seconds*

One more time, find the still point at the end of it. Rest there.

*Pause 45 seconds*

When you are ready, let the next swing carry you out.

*Pause 15 seconds*

---

## After

You can find a still point any time, in about four seconds: at the bottom of one breath, or in the step before one answer. It does not need a cushion, a quiet room or a good day.

Return to this sitting when everything feels like one long push with no turning in it. Return when you have worked through every pause you were given. Return when the rhythms around you are loud, and you want to remember you have your own.

---

## Where It Comes From

The picture is Itzhak Bentov's. An engineer who invented the steerable heart catheter, he spent his last years describing consciousness in his own trade's terms: oscillators, rhythm, rest. In *Stalking the Wild Pendulum* (1977) he wrote that everything moves in a "Morse code of action and rest". He also described an experiment with a clock's second hand in a meditative state. This sitting borrows the swing, the rest and the clock. It does not borrow his further claims about what happens in the stillness, which have not been tested.

The even pace near five seconds in and five out comes from later research on slow breathing and heart rhythm, by Evgeny Vaschillo, Paul Lehrer and others, [summarized here](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00756/full). It is offered as a comfortable pace, not a treatment. Each body's best pace differs.
