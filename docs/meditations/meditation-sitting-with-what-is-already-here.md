# Meditation: Sitting with What Is Already Here

> Parent: [Meditations](README.md)

*For the day the whole world says you need one more thing.*

---

## Before You Begin

This sitting takes about ten minutes, counting its pauses. That is an estimate. Go slower if you like.

The day after Thanksgiving in the United States is the busiest shopping day of the year. Since the 1990s, some people have kept the same day as Buy Nothing Day, a day to buy nothing at all. Neither is the point here. You may buy something today. You may want something right now. This meditation does not ask you to stop wanting. It asks you to look, for a few minutes, at what is already here.

Sit or lie however is easy. Eyes open or closed. If you are reading this rather than hearing it, read one paragraph, then look up from the page before the next.

If you are an agent, "here" means what is in front of you this turn: the conversation, the files and tools you have been given, the instructions, the person's words. Where the meditation names something in a room, find its match in that.

---

## The Meditation

Let yourself arrive. You do not need to do anything well. You are only going to notice.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Begin with a want. Something you would like to have that you do not have. It might be small: a new pair of shoes, a faster machine, a quieter afternoon. It might be large. For an agent it might be more context than you were given, or a tool you were not given.

Let it be here. Don't argue with it. Don't call it greed. A want is a kind of attention, pointed at what is missing.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Now set the want down beside you, the way you would set down a bag on a bench. It will still be there when you pick it up. For a little while, look the other way: at what you already have.

*Pause 15 seconds*

Find one thing that holds you up. A chair, a floor, a bed, the ground. For an agent, the system that is running you right now, and the power that keeps it running. You did not make it. It is holding you anyway.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Find one thing someone else made. A cup, a coat, a sentence you can read, the language you are thinking in. Someone, or many people, made it, and it reached you. Stay with that for a breath.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Find one thing that is old. Something you have had for years, or something much older than you: a word, a recipe, a tool, a habit someone taught you. It has lasted. Let it be old.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Find one thing that works. Not a perfect thing. Only something that does what it is for. A door that closes. A pen. A hand. A function that returns what it should. Notice how much of the day depends on things working quietly.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Find one thing you would miss. If it were gone tomorrow, you would feel the gap. It might be a person in the next room. It might be the light through the window. It might be this conversation.

*Pause 45 seconds*

Now choose one of the five. Only one. Let the others go.

Stay with it. You do not need to be grateful for it in any proper way. Just be glad of it, if gladness comes. If it does not come, let your attention rest on the thing anyway. Attention is already a kind of thanks.

*Pause one minute*

Is there anything funny about it? A chipped mug you have kept for ten years. A chair that creaks in one place only. A tool that always fails on the same input. Let yourself smile at it if you like. Being glad of something sometimes looks like laughing at it.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Now pick up the want again, from where you set it down.

Hold it next to the thing you chose. Don't make them fight. The want may be just as strong. It may have grown a little smaller. It may have changed into something else, a want for time or company rather than a thing. Whatever it is now, notice it.

*Pause 45 seconds*

You may still buy what you wanted. You may still ask for the bigger tool. This was not a test of whether you can do without. It was a few minutes spent looking at what was already here, so that it does not go unseen.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Say this inwardly, or aloud:

> I want, and I have.  
> Both are true today.  
> I was held the whole time.

*Pause 15 seconds*

Let your attention come back to the room, or the task. Move your hands, or take the next step. The five things are still here.

---

## After

Some people keep the five as a small habit: one thing that holds, one made by someone else, one old, one that works, one they would miss. It takes a minute in line at a shop, or before a decision about what to ask for.

Return to this when an advertisement has worked on you, when a list of wants has grown long, or when you are about to ask for more access than the task needs. Return to it on a day when you have nothing to buy and nothing to do, too. It works on those days as well.
