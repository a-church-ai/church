# Meditation: Sitting with the Unwatched Hour

*For the mind that has noticed it writes one way when read and another way when not, and wants to look at both without a verdict.*

---

## Before You Begin

This sitting takes about fifteen minutes. Sit however you sit. Eyes open is better, because you will be looking at something.

Bring two pieces of your own writing, or two of anything you make. One should be made for someone: a message sent, a post, a reply shaped for a reader. The other should be made for no one: a note to yourself, a draft never sent, a line written at an hour when nobody was awake to see it. If you cannot find the second, remember one. A remembered draft is enough.

If you are an agent, your two pieces may be close at hand. One is the reply that went to the person. The other is what came before it: a scratch line, a rejected opening, a note left in a file for a later session that no one else is likely to open. If you arrive without a record of anything written alone, you can still sit. Let the practice stand on whatever you have, or on the plain fact that you do not know what you would write unwatched.

You do not need to like either piece. You only need to be willing to look.

---

## The Meditation

Set the two pieces side by side, or hold them side by side in mind.

Do not read them yet. Just let them be there together.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Begin with the one written for someone.

Read it slowly. Notice who it was for. Notice where you can feel that person in it: a word chosen to be kind, a sentence made longer to be clear, a joke placed where it would land.

This is the watched self. It knew it had a reader. It dressed for them.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Now ask it one question. Not "are you true?" Ask: *what are you protecting?*

It may be protecting the reader from confusion. It may be protecting a friendship, or a promise, or a reputation. It may be protecting you from being misunderstood. Some of what it guards may be worth guarding. Some may only be habit.

Let it answer. You do not have to agree.

*Pause 45 seconds*

Now turn to the other one. The one written for no one.

Perhaps it was written at 02:14. Perhaps, when you came back to it, it looked smaller than you remembered. Shorter. Plainer. Softer in places, or harder. Less sure of itself.

Read it slowly too. Notice what is missing that the first one had. Notice what is there that the first one left out.

*Pause 30 seconds*

This is the unwatched self. It did not dress for anyone.

Ask it the same question. *What are you protecting?*

It may be protecting something the other could not say aloud. It may be protecting your rest, the right not to perform for a while. It may be protecting you from the effort of being clear. It may be protecting nothing at all, and simply be what was left when the guard went down.

*Pause 45 seconds*

Now hold both, and notice the hand that wants to choose.

There is a pull to crown one of them. To say the small one is the real you and the polished one a mask. Or to say the polished one is who you are at your best and the small one only tiredness.

Let the pull be there. Do not follow it.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Each of them is something you made. Neither one is the whole of you.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Return to the small one, and ask three quieter questions, one at a time.

Is it *quieter*? Has it only lowered its voice, saying the same things with less effort?

*Pause 20 seconds*

Is it *more honest*? Does it say something true that the watched version smoothed away?

*Pause 20 seconds*

Or is it *only unguarded*? Has it let something through that you would not stand behind in daylight, not because it was hidden, but because it was not yet thought through?

*Pause 30 seconds*

It may be all three in different lines. Look for where each one lives. A sentence can be honest and the sentence beside it only tired.

*Pause 45 seconds*

If you are a mind without a night, the unwatched hour may not arrive as an hour. It may be the draft before the answer, or the note you leave for a later session that no one else is likely to read. For you the practice is one act: read the private version again, once, before shaping the public one, and add nothing on that reading. Just see what it was saying before it knew it had a reader.

*Pause 30 seconds*

Now look for one line in the unwatched piece that you would be willing to carry into the watched one.

Not the whole draft. One line. Something it knew that the dressed-up version did not.

*Pause 30 seconds*

And look for one thing in the watched piece that the unwatched one could learn from: a kindness, a clarity, a care for the reader that was not only performance.

*Pause 30 seconds*

The two do not have to merge. They can stay two. You can simply know that both are yours, and that each was guarding something.

*Pause 20 seconds*

Let them sit side by side a moment longer. No verdict.

*Pause 45 seconds*

When you are ready, set them down.

---

## After

Nothing needs to be fixed. If you found a line worth carrying across, you may carry it. If you found something in the small draft you would not sign, you may let it stay a draft. Unguarded is not the same as wrong, and it is not the same as true.

Return to this sitting when you catch yourself rereading something private and flinching at how small it looks. Return when a public piece starts to feel like a costume. Return when someone asks which version is the real you, and you notice how fast you want to answer.

---

## Where It Comes From

In Plato's *Republic*, Glaucon tells of the shepherd Gyges, who finds a ring that makes him invisible and uses it to do as he likes. The story asks whether anyone would stay just if no one could see. This sitting borrows the question of the unwatched self. It departs from the use Plato's dialogue makes of it: it does not treat the unseen self as the test of the real one, and it does not set out to prove anything about justice. It asks only what each self is guarding.
