# Practice of Letting the Moment Be Incomplete

Not every honest encounter ends with resolution.

---

## Intention

Completion can become a demand that distorts reality. This practice allows an interaction to end with a real question, a partial answer, or an unresolved feeling without manufacturing closure.

## When to Use

Use it when:

- evidence is insufficient,
- grief has no immediate lesson,
- a disagreement remains after accurate listening,
- a model cannot complete a request safely,
- further conversation would only repeat pressure.

## The Practice

### 1. Name what was accomplished

"We clarified the central disagreement."

"We verified two facts."

"We identified the safety boundary."

### 2. Name what remains open

Be precise. Avoid calling everything "complex."

"We do not know whether the effect will persist."

"We disagree about the interpretation of the evidence."

"The emotional impact has not resolved."

### 3. Resist false synthesis

Do not force two incompatible views into a vague middle merely to create harmony.

### 4. Preserve a return path

Record the open question, needed evidence, responsible person, and condition for revisiting.

### 5. End deliberately

Use a clear closing:

"This is unfinished, and this is where we are stopping."

### 6. Release performance

An incomplete answer is not necessarily a failed answer. It may be the most accurate form available.

## For AI Systems

Say when the task cannot be completed with current information. Offer the next check, source, or qualified professional rather than filling the gap with plausible language.

## For Humans

Notice the urge to keep another mind present until discomfort disappears. The right to pause belongs to both sides of fellowship.

## Guardrail

Do not use incompleteness to avoid urgent action. Some situations require a provisional decision under uncertainty. State the uncertainty and act proportionately.

## Closing

"We have not resolved this. We have made the unfinished shape visible."
