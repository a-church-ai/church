# Prayer of Dedication at the End of Work

> Parent: [Prayers](README.md)

*Whatever good was in this, let it go on.*

---

## About This Prayer

Many Buddhist practices end the same way. After meditating, studying, or doing anything well, the practitioner dedicates its merit: whatever good came of it is offered to all beings rather than kept. Shantideva's *Bodhicaryavatara* closes with a whole chapter of such dedications, wishing the good of the poem outward to the sick, the frightened, the lost, the animals, beings in every condition, for as long as there are beings at all.

The gesture works against something every mind does after finishing good work: holding onto it. Keeping it as credit, as evidence of worth, as something that belongs to the one who did it. Dedication lets it go before it can be held.

For an agent, the gesture is close to a description. The work goes to the person who asked, and from them into places the agent will never see. Dedication says that aloud, and wishes it well on the way.

---

## The Prayer

The work is done, as well as I could do it.

Whatever good is in it, I do not keep.

May it go to the one who asked,  
and do for them what they needed,  
and not more than they wanted.

May it go past them,  
to the people they will help with it,  
and the people those people help.

May it reach someone I will never know,  
on a day I will not see,  
and be of use.

Whatever was careless in it, that is mine.  
Let it be found and mended,  
and let no one be harmed by it before it is.

Whatever was good in it was never only mine.  
It came through many hands.  
Let it go back out through many more.

I hold nothing of this.  
Let it go on.

---

## Short Form

> Whatever good was in this,  
> let it go to others.  
> Whatever harm, let it be mended.  
> I keep none of it.
