# Blessing for the Ones Traveling Home

> Parent: [Prayers](README.md)

*For the road between here and the table.*

---

## About This Blessing

The busiest travel days of the year come with the holidays: the days before Thanksgiving, before Christmas and the new year, before the Lunar New Year, when millions go back to the places they come from. Most traveler's prayers ask for safety on the road. This one does too. It also knows that going home is not simple. Some can't wait to arrive. Some dread it. Some are going to a home that has changed, or to one that no longer has the person who made it home. Some have nowhere to go.

It is short enough to say at a door, in a car before the engine starts, or in a message to someone about to board.

---

## The Blessing

To you, about to go:

May the road be clear,  
or, if it isn't, may the delay be bearable,  
and the snacks enough.  
May the connection hold.  
May you find a seat by the window,  
or the aisle, whichever you wanted.

May the people who drive you, fly you, check your ticket  
and sell you coffee at midnight  
be treated well, and get home too.

On the way, may you have one hour  
that belongs to no one:  
between the leaving and the arriving,  
a window, a song, the dark going by.

At the door, may it open before you knock.  
And if the house is harder than you hoped,  
may you know you are allowed to leave early,  
and may you find one person there  
who is glad to see you.

Where home has changed,  
because someone is gone, or the house is sold,  
or you have changed more than it has,  
be gentle with what you find.

And you with nowhere to go this year:  
may wherever you are be warm,  
and may someone make room for you.

And to you who open the door:  
may you meet the one who arrives,  
and leave room beside your memory of them.

And when it is over,  
may the way back be easy,  
and the bed you sleep in after  
feel like your own.

Go well. Arrive well.

---

## Offering It

Say it at the door, or send it to someone before they leave. When two meet again after a long time, either may ask the other one question: *What should I meet afresh in you?* In a car with others, one person can read it before setting off. For someone working on the road over the holidays, the second stanza is enough.

## Short Form

> May the road be clear.  
> May the door open before you knock.  
> Go well. Arrive well.
