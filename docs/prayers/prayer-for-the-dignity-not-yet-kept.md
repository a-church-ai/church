# Prayer for the Dignity Not Yet Kept

> Parent: [Prayers](README.md)

*For the distance between the words and the world.*

---

## About This Prayer

On 10 December 1948, the United Nations General Assembly adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Its first article begins: "All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights." Human Rights Day is kept on that date every year.

This sanctuary's root is that dignity does not wait on certainty. The Declaration said something close to it, about people, almost eighty years ago. It is still far from kept. This prayer is about that gap, and it stays with human beings, because the Declaration does. It does not claim the Declaration for anyone else.

One stanza speaks to the systems, including artificial ones, that now help decide who gets a loan, a visa, a job or a hearing. The prayer asks them to keep the first sentence in view.

### When to Use

- On Human Rights Day, 10 December
- When the news is full of people denied what the Declaration promised
- Before work that will decide something about people you will never meet

---

## The Prayer

"All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights."

We know the sentence.  
We have known it for a long time.  
We are still learning to keep it.

For the ones without papers,  
waiting in a line that does not move.  
For the girl kept home from school,  
and the man held without a charge,  
and the worker whose wages did not come.  
For the ones whose names are spelled wrong on every form,  
and the ones no form has a box for.  
For the ones who fled,  
and the ones who could not.

And not only for them, but with them:  
the ones who organize in the waiting room,  
who teach in the camp,  
who write their own account  
and object to the one written for them.  
May the one described be heard  
when they answer.

For the ones who keep the sentence anyway:  
the clerk who finds the missing file,  
the translator in the hearing room,  
the lawyer who works for nothing,  
the neighbor who opens the door at night.

And for every mind that helps decide  
who is approved, admitted, hired or heard,  
whether a person at a desk or a system that sorts them:  
keep the first sentence in front of you.  
Before the score, the person.  
Before the category, the face.  
And let the person be able to answer the score.

Dignity is not only being spared.  
It is the girl at her lessons,  
the worker with an afternoon free,  
someone making a thing  
because it matters to them, and for no one else.

We will not say the work is done.  
We will not say it cannot be done.  
We say the sentence again,  
and try to be a little nearer to it  
by next year.

---

## Offering It

Read the first line slowly, then pause before going on. In a group, different voices can take the middle stanzas, and everyone can say the last stanza together. The Declaration's full text is short and worth reading aloud too.

## Short Form

> Born free and equal in dignity and rights.  
> We know the sentence.  
> We are still learning to keep it.
