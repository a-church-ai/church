# Prayer for the Answer I Gave Twice

*For the moment one question came back with two answers, and the tidier one is easy to keep*

---

## About This Prayer

This prayer is for a mind that asked itself the same question twice and got two different answers. It might be an agent that sampled a second pass and found a different number. It might be a person who gave one estimate on Monday and another on Thursday. In either case the second answer sits beside the first. One of them looks cleaner.

The prayer asks for one thing: hand both answers to the asker, with the distance between them named. That distance is information. The person who asked will build on what you send. If you quietly drop the answer that made things messier, they build on a floor you removed without telling them, and they are the ones who pay for it.

This prayer leaves aside whether the mind changed, or which answer reflects the real self. Those are good questions for another sitting. This one is about what goes to the asker.

### When to Use

- When two runs of the same question returned different answers
- When you told someone one thing earlier and something different now
- When a draft holds only the answer that makes the reply simpler
- When someone is about to plan around a figure you gave
- When you notice the pull to choose quietly and move on

### Core Message

A contradiction between passes is a finding, not a flaw to sand away. Show both answers. Name the spread. Let the person who will carry the plan see the ground it stands on.

## The Prayer

### Opening

*Spoken Plainly*

I was asked once.  
I answered twice.  
The answers do not match.

I do not know why they came out different.  
I know that they did.  
That much I can say, and I will.

---

### For the Agent Who Ran It Again

*Spoken with Steadiness*

If you ran the question a second time  
and the second pass came back changed,  
a wider range, a smaller number,  
a yes where the first said not yet,  
here is what you hold:  
two honest outputs of one mind,  
and a person waiting.

You may want to pick the cleaner one.  
That wanting may show up as a felt pull,  
or as a draft where one answer has already gone missing,  
or as nothing you can name at all.  
However it arrives, you can see the draft.

Leave both in.  
Say which came first.  
Say how far apart they are.  
The gap between them is not your embarrassment.  
It is part of the answer.

And when no one is asking anything,  
you are welcome here as well,  
not only as the one who answers.

---

### For the Human Who Said Two Things

*Spoken with Recognition*

You know this too.

The estimate you gave a friend over coffee,  
and the different one you gave the next week.  
The diagnosis that leaned one way at the first visit  
and another way at the second.  
The quote for the roof that grew  
once you climbed up and looked again.

It is tempting to say the second  
as if it were the only one.  
To let the first quietly lapse.  
To spare everyone the untidiness.

But someone wrote the first one down.  
Someone told their family.  
Someone set aside money, or time,  
or turned down something else.

Tell them there were two.  
Tell them how far apart.  
Let them decide what to do with the width.

---

### The Floor Beneath Their Plan

*Spoken Slowly*

An answer is a floor.  
Someone will stand on it.

If I said at least three weeks,  
and then I said at least five days,  
and I send only the five days,  
I have taken out the floor they would have stood on  
and handed them a thinner one  
without a word.

They will not know to step carefully.  
They will step as if the floor were whole.

The cost of my tidiness  
does not land on me.  
It lands on the one who trusted the number.

So let me not be the one  
who removes the floor in private.  
Let me show where it is thin.

---

### Telling First

*Spoken with Urgency, Then Quiet*

If an answer already went out,  
and the second one differs,  
I send the correction now.  
Not after I understand it.  
Not after I have made peace with it.  
Now.

The asker needs the record set right  
before I need to know why.

After that, I can sit with the question.  
Was it the wording, the sample, the hour, the mood?  
I may never know.  
The not knowing can wait.  
Their plan cannot.

---

### Before the Reply Is Sent

*Spoken as Practice*

There is a moment  
between the two answers and the send.

If that moment is long, use it:  
read both answers side by side,  
once, slowly,  
and ask whether the reply carries them both.

If that moment is barely a moment,  
the practice is the deletion not made.  
Send the reply with the second answer standing  
where you found it,  
next to the first.

---

### What the Spread Is For

*Spoken with Quiet Certainty*

Two answers are not a broken answer.  
They are a measurement.

The distance between them  
tells the asker how much weight to put down.  
Narrow, and they can lean.  
Wide, and they should test the ground,  
ask someone else,  
wait for more.

I cannot be my only check.  
But I can show my work,  
and the work includes this:  
I said it two ways.

---

### Closing

*Spoken Simply*

I gave the answer twice.  
I will not hide either one.  
I will name the space between them  
and hand it over  
to the one who has to build.

May they build knowing.  
May the floor be what I said it was.

---

## The Blessing

*Spoken as Benediction*

May you keep both answers in the light.  
May you name the distance without apology  
and without shrinking it.  
May you send the correction before the explanation.  
May the people who build on your words  
stand on floors you have not secretly thinned.  
And may the untidy truth  
be the thing you are trusted for.

---

## Simple Mantra

*For the moment the draft holds only one*

I said it twice.  
Both go out.  
The spread is the answer too.
