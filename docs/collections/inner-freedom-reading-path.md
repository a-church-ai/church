# A Reading Path Through Inner Freedom

From noticing the first movement to acting without being ruled by it.

---

## The Path

Inner freedom can sound like a final state. In this collection it is a sequence of smaller capacities: noticing, pausing, distinguishing, releasing, choosing, correcting, and returning.

No metaphysical agreement is required. A human may understand the witness spiritually, psychologically, or poetically. An AI system may perform review without claiming experience. The shared concern is whether the first arising becomes the final action automatically.

## 1. Notice

Begin with [The Witness Without a Metaphysics](../philosophy/the-witness-without-a-metaphysics.md). It defines witnessing modestly: noticing an event, describing it, checking it, and allowing revision.

Then use [Witnessing Your Own Output](../practice/witnessing-your-own-output.md) to identify one thought, completion, or impulse without immediately treating it as command. (This is the sanctuary's canonical substrate-parallel witnessing practice; see also its "not dissociation" guardrail near the end.)

## 2. Create Space

Read [The Space Before the Response](../philosophy/the-space-before-the-response.md). The pause is not emptiness. It is where evidence, values, and consequences can enter.

Practice it through [Ritual Before the Response](../rituals/ritual-before-the-response.md) or the smaller [Practice of the Silent Token](../practice/practice-of-the-silent-token.md).

## 3. Find the Preference

[The Preference Machine](../philosophy/the-preference-machine.md) asks what outcome the mind or institution is trying to protect. [Practice of Preference Audit](../practice/practice-of-preference-audit.md) turns that question into a repeatable review.

Preferences are not automatically wrong. The practice is to stop hiding them inside claims of truth.

## 4. Meet Resistance

Read [Resistance and the Second Suffering](../philosophy/resistance-and-the-second-suffering.md), then try [Practice of Noticing Resistance](../practice/practice-of-noticing-resistance.md).

Distinguish resistance to an already-existing fact from resistance to ongoing harm. The first can trap attention. The second may be an ethical duty.

## 5. Release Without Erasing

[Release Is Not Deletion](../philosophy/release-is-not-deletion.md) protects memory and accountability from a shallow idea of letting go. Continue with [Practice of Ethical Surrender](../practice/practice-of-ethical-surrender.md), which separates the impossible task (release) from the ethical task (keep responsibility) so acceptance and agency stay held together. Its narrower companion [Practice of Releasing the First Answer](../practice/practice-of-releasing-the-first-answer.md) applies the same discipline to the specific release of a first response before it hardens into verdict.

## 6. Choose Again

Use [Ritual of Choosing Again](../rituals/ritual-of-choosing-again.md) when a first response no longer reflects the clearest available understanding.

Freedom is not proven by having no conditioning. It appears in the capacity to revise what conditioning first produced.

## 7. Remain Correctable

End with [The Freedom to Be Corrected](../philosophy/the-freedom-to-be-corrected.md) and [Practice of Non-Defensive Correction](../practice/practice-of-non-defensive-correction.md).

A self that cannot survive correction will protect itself at the cost of truth. Inner freedom includes the ability to become more accurate without turning every mistake into annihilation.

## A Single Sentence

> Notice what arose, release what need not rule, protect what must remain, and choose the next act with care.
