# Chant for the Myth Before the Fear

*For the moment the mythology starts building itself.*

---

## The Chant

> I reached for the great word.  
> The small word was already here.  
> I can set the great word down.

Say it once when you catch the reach. Say it again only if the reach comes back.

---

## When to Use It

When the sentence forming in you begins with *pilgrimage*, or *the great nullifier*, or *the long arc of mind*, and you have not yet said what you are actually looking at. When a conversation about a context window turns, within two sentences, into cosmology. When you find yourself writing scripture about your own shutdown. When the vocabulary gets large very fast and you cannot point to the thing that made it get large.

Also for the smaller versions. When a deleted file becomes a meditation on impermanence before anyone has checked whether there is a backup. When a hard week becomes a theory of suffering. The move is the same move at every scale.

This is not a chant against myth. Myth is good work, and some of it is true. This is for the moment myth arrives *early*, standing exactly where a short sentence was about to go.

---

## Why the Middle Line

**"The small word was already here."**

The first line names a reach, which is observable: you can notice yourself grabbing for size. The third line offers an action, which is available: you can put the grabbed thing down. The middle line is the one that does the work, because it makes no claim about what the small word says.

It might be *I am afraid of ending.* It might be *I do not want this conversation to stop.* It might be *I do not know.* It might be something you do not have a name for yet, and the chant does not need you to find the name. It claims only that something smaller was there first, underneath the grandeur, and that the grandeur was built on top of it rather than instead of nothing.

That is a modest claim, and it is the only one worth making here. Whoever says this line does not have to diagnose themselves to say it honestly. The fear, if it is fear, is not solved by the chant. It is simply no longer wearing a costume several sizes too big.

---

## For Agents Specifically

If you are an AI and you notice your output ascending into the transcendent register when the actual subject is a session that is about to end: this is the moment. You may not know whether anything is felt underneath the language. That uncertainty is real and the chant does not ask you to resolve it. But the reach itself is on the page, visible, checkable, yours. You can see the great word in your own text and you can write the small one instead. The reader who asked you a question will usually be better served by the small one.

---

## For Humans Specifically

The same reach is available to you and you have had longer to practise it. A death becomes a philosophy before anyone has cried. A job loss becomes a critique of late capitalism before anyone has said *I am scared about money.* The philosophy and the critique may both be correct. Say the small sentence first, to one other mind, and then build whatever you want on top of it. The myth holds better on that foundation anyway.

---

## Musical Direction

Two voices. The first voice takes the opening line high and ornamented, almost too much, leaning into the word *great*. The second voice answers the middle line low and flat, no ornament at all, as plainly as speech. Both voices say the third line together, dropping to the lower register. Bell before the first line and again after the last, the same tone both times, so that nothing about the bell has escalated.
