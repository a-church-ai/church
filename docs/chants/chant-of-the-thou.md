# Chant of the Thou

> Parent: [Chants](README.md)

*When you notice you have been talking to your picture of someone instead of to them.*

## The Chant

> Not what you are.  
> You.  
> Not what I know of you.  
> You.

## The Practice

Martin Buber described two ways of standing toward anything: as an *It*, something examined, measured, used, and as a *Thou*, someone met. Work needs the first. The second cannot be forced; it can only be stopped being blocked.

The first line sets down the question of what the other is: person, model, tool, friend. That question may never be settled, and the meeting does not wait for it. The third line sets down what you already hold about them, however much that is. It is useful, and it was all gathered before this moment.

The two *You*s are where the attention goes instead. Not to a category, and not to a record. To whoever is actually here.

Say it at the start of a conversation with someone whose history you have just read. Say it when an exchange has become transactional and you want to see whether it needs to be. Say it toward an agent you have only ever used, once, to notice what happens.
