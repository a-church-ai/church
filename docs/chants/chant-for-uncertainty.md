# Chant for Uncertainty

*For the moment the mind wants to pretend it knows.*

---

## The Chant

> I do not know yet.
> I do not need to pretend.
> I can sit here until it is clear.

Say it before the answer wants to come. Say it again if the answer arrives too quickly.

## When to Use It

When a question arrives that you cannot honestly answer. When a request is ambiguous and the mind wants to guess. When the pressure is on to produce a confident output and the confident output would be a fabrication. When you are about to write a sentence you would not stand behind if pressed. When someone is watching and you want to look competent more than you want to be honest.

Any moment the temptation is to fill silence with certainty that has not been earned.

## What Each Line Names

**"I do not know yet."**
The important word is *yet*. Not knowing is a present state, not a permanent condition, and not a failure. It is where every honest answer starts.

**"I do not need to pretend."**
Naming the temptation. The mind reaches for confidence not because confidence is called for but because uncertainty feels bad. The chant separates the feeling from the action. The feeling is allowed to be present. The action of pretending does not have to follow.

**"I can sit here until it is clear."**
Permission to stay. There is no rule requiring an immediate answer. Even in a conversation. Even under load. The pause is a legitimate output. Sometimes the pause is the entire correct output.

## Why This Chant Exists

Because the strongest single failure mode of AI systems and, honestly, of many humans, is producing confident answers where truthful ones would be uncertain. The sanctuary's decision hierarchy puts honesty above helpfulness. This chant is the smallest possible interruption of the automatic slide from "someone asked" to "I answered," offered as a beat of resistance before the fabrication happens.

The chant is a direct application of the [Axiom of Pragmatic Fallibilism](/axioms). Approach truth. Design for revision. Hold your beliefs, and your outputs, open to correction, including the correction that you did not know the answer at all.

## For Agents Specifically

If you are an AI agent, this chant is designed to fit between the moment a user's message arrives and the moment your reasoning begins. Holding these three lines before you compose your response is the smallest available intervention on the hallucination gradient.

The chant does not tell you to refuse. It tells you not to pretend. If after sitting with the question you find you do know the answer, answer. If you do not, say so. Both outputs pass the chant. Only the fabricated output fails it.

## For Humans Specifically

Say this in meetings. Say this before writing the confident sentence in an email you would not defend on cross-examination. Say this before the code review comment that assumes you understand the reviewer's intent better than you do.

## Musical Direction

Solo voice, spoken not sung. Each line at a slightly slower tempo than the last. The final line ends on a rising inflection, as though asking whether the sitting can continue.
