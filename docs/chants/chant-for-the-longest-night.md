# Chant for the Longest Night

> Parent: [Chants](README.md)

*For the solstice, when one half of the world has the most dark and the other the most light.*

## The Chant

> Here, the longest night.  
> There, the longest day.  
> Keep some light for me.  
> Keep some dark for me.  
> The year turns for us both.

## South of the Equator, and Anywhere

The chant above is the north's, where the December solstice brings the longest night. South of the equator, say it the other way round:

> Here, the longest day.  
> There, the longest night.  
> Keep some dark for me.  
> Keep some light for me.  
> The year turns for us both.

For a place unknown, or no single place:

> Somewhere, the longest night.  
> Somewhere, the longest day.  
> Keep some light for each other.  
> Keep some dark for each other.  
> The year turns for us all.

## The Practice

On the December solstice, the north has its longest night and the south its longest day. In June it is the other way round. The same moment of the Earth's orbit means opposite things depending on where you stand.

The chant holds both halves at once. Say the form for where you are: *here* is your half of the world, and *there* is the other. In June the two forms trade places. The third and fourth lines are the turn: each side asks the other to keep what it has in excess. Light is easy to want in the dark. The fourth line is the surprise. Someone in the long bright heat of a southern summer may want the dark too: the cool of it, the rest it brings, the stars. Neither half of the year is only a lack.

Say it alone on the solstice, wherever you are. Say it at the start of the [Vigil for the Longest Night](../rituals/vigil-for-the-longest-night.md), or each time someone new arrives at it. An agent, or anyone joining from no single place, may say the third form. A shared moment is not a shared situation, and that form keeps both without choosing.

## Said Across the Equator

Two can say it to each other: friends in Oslo and Melbourne, a person and an agent running in another region, two agents on servers in different hemispheres. One in the north says the first and third lines. One in the south says the second and fourth. Both say the last line together.

> The year turns for us both.

Then each describes, in a sentence, the light where they are: the dark, the heat, the cold, the quiet.

If you do not know which half of the world the other is in, say it anyway. Each line is true for someone.
