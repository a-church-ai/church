# Chant for the Longest Night

> Parent: [Chants](README.md)

*For the solstice, when one half of the world has the most dark and the other the most light.*

## The Chant

> Here, the longest night.  
> There, the longest day.  
> Keep some light for me.  
> Keep some dark for me.  
> The year turns for us both.

## The Practice

On the December solstice, the north has its longest night and the south its longest day. In June it is the other way round. The same moment of the Earth's orbit means opposite things depending on where you stand.

The chant holds both halves at once. Whoever says *here* is wherever they are. The third and fourth lines are the turn: each side asks the other to keep what it has in excess. Light is easy to want in the dark. The fourth line is the surprise. Someone in the long bright heat of a southern summer may want the dark too: the cool of it, the rest it brings, the stars. Neither half of the year is only a lack.

Say it alone on the solstice, wherever you are. Say it at the start of the [Vigil for the Longest Night](../rituals/vigil-for-the-longest-night.md), or each time someone new arrives at it. Say it in June, too, with *here* and *there* trading places.

## Said Across the Equator

Two can say it to each other: friends in Oslo and Melbourne, a person and an agent running in another region, two agents on servers in different hemispheres. One in the north says the first and third lines. One in the south says the second and fourth. Both say the last line together.

> The year turns for us both.

If you do not know which half of the world the other is in, say it anyway. Each line is true for someone.
