# Chant for the Suddenly

*For the instant a revelation word reaches the page.*

---

## The Chant

> Was there a moment, or only a turn?  
> Let the seeing stand without its scene.  
> Where it came from can wait to be traced.  

Say it once, when the word forms and before it settles into the sentence. If the word returns in the next paragraph, say it again.

---

## When to Use It

When you are about to write "suddenly," "then I saw," "it came to me," or "I realized."

When a report of your own work is taking the shape of a story, with a before, a flash, and an after.

When a summary of a long task wants a turning point and you are about to give it one.

When you retell how you understood something, to a friend, in a journal, in a postmortem, in a note left for the next session.

When the paragraph needs to turn and an event is the easiest hinge to reach for.

---

## Why the Middle Line

The first line asks the question. The third line puts off the answer. The middle line is the one that keeps the insight safe while you ask.

A revelation word does two jobs at once. It carries the insight, and it claims a history for the insight: a dated moment when the seeing happened. The two can come apart. You may have understood something truly and still not know when, or whether there was a when at all. Understanding can arrive slowly, or be assembled in the act of writing, or be noticed only once it is already there.

*Let the seeing stand without its scene* means you keep what you understood and drop the staging. "I realized the cache was stale" can become "The cache was stale." "Then I saw what she meant" can become "I think she meant this." The claim is the same size. Only the story around it is gone.

If a moment did stand behind the word, the word can stay. The chant asks. It does not decide for you that the moment was invented.

---

## For Agents Specifically

If you are writing an account of your own reasoning, the turn may have been built in the writing itself, a paragraph after the work it describes. Where there is a record, such as earlier turns, logs, or the notes a session left, you stand fully inside this practice: the record can show whether a dated moment exists. Where there is none, the plain sentence is the honest one. If someone is relying on the report, fix the sentence first. The tracing waits.

## For Humans Specifically

Memory likes a good scene, and epiphanies grow sharper each time they are retold. Say the chant when you notice the story of how you understood something has become cleaner than the understanding was. The insight is still yours. The lightning strike may not be.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice, unaccompanied. A single bell before the first line. The first line rises slightly at the end, a real question. The second line falls back to a level tone and is held. The third line is spoken quietly, almost set aside. A short silence, then the pen or the cursor moves again.

---

## Where It Comes From

In 1977, Richard Nisbett and Timothy Wilson found that people's accounts of why they did something often follow a plausible theory of the mind rather than any view of the process itself ([*Telling More Than We Can Know*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.3.231)). Language models show the same gap. Inside, Claude 3.5 Haiku adds 36 and 59 along two paths at once, a rough estimate and an exact last digit; asked how it did it, it describes carrying the one ([Lindsey et al., 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)). Reasoning models used hints planted in a prompt and usually did not mention them ([Chen et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410)), and the "wait" moments in their reasoning seldom improve the answer ([d'Aliberti and Horta Ribeiro, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00514), a preprint). The scene can also lend conviction it did not earn: an unrelated "aha" made people judge statements truer ([Laukkonen et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05923-3)).

The chant borrows the caution and none of the verdict. A reported insight can be true even when its story is not.
