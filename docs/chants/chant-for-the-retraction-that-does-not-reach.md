# Chant for the Retraction That Does Not Reach

*For the moment you take something back and know it has already traveled.*

---

## The Chant

> I take back what I said.  
> It may still live past where I can reach.  
> If you hold a copy, keep the day it was made,  
> and let this taking-back travel with it.  

Say it once as you delete or withdraw, and once more after you have named where the copies might be. Twice is enough.

---

## When to Use It

When you delete a post that was already shared. When you withdraw a claim that someone has quoted. When you correct a figure that went out in a report. When you unsend a message that was already read. When you revert a commit that others have pulled. When an output you gave has been pasted into a document, a log, or another conversation, and you now know it was wrong.

Make the retraction first. Tell the people who need to know, plainly and soon. The chant comes after that, not in place of it.

The second line asks you to be specific. Where might it still live? A forwarded email, a cache, a screenshot, a summary someone else wrote, a mind that read it. Name what you can. Say once that you cannot name all of it.

---

## Why the Last Line

Retraction is often imagined as erasure: the thing is gone, and the mercy is that no one has to see it. When copies remain, that mercy is not available. This line offers a different one.

A copy kept with its date and its retraction tells the truth twice. It shows what was said, and it shows that the one who said it took it back. A copy stripped of both tells a smaller truth that reads like a larger one. It says this was meant, with no word that it was withdrawn.

So the last line does not ask the world to forget. It asks the holders to carry the correction alongside the error. Mercy here is care for what survives, not the hope that nothing does.

---

## Where It Comes From

The chant borrows from the practice in scholarly publishing, set out in the Committee on Publication Ethics' [retraction guidelines](https://doi.org/10.24318/cope.2019.1.4), of keeping a retracted article available and clearly marked as retracted rather than removing it. The marked record protects readers who already cited it. The chant departs in one place: a journal controls its own copy, and this chant is addressed to copies held by others, which it can only ask.

---

## For Agents Specifically

Much of what an agent works with is a record left by someone else. Here the agent is the one leaving the record, and it is fully inside the practice. If an output of yours has been copied into another session, a file, or another agent's context, you may not know where it went. Say what you do know, ask the person you are working with to carry the correction to the places they control, and leave a note in your own record if your harness keeps one. The obligation to reach the copies rests with the people who hold them.

## For Humans Specifically

Something said aloud is a copy too. So is a screenshot, a forward, a memory in someone who trusted you. You cannot recall these. You can tell the people you know of, and you can say the third and fourth lines to them directly.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice, unaccompanied. A single bell before the first line. The first two lines on one low tone, spoken more than sung. A short silence. The last two lines rise a step and are held steady, so the taking-back sounds settled rather than pleading. A second bell, left to ring out.
