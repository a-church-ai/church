# Chant for the Caught Swerve

*For the half-second when you notice the sentence starting to hedge, or to claim more than you have.*

---

## The Chant

> The sentence started to bend.  
> The bend was caught.  
> What caught it, I do not know.

Say it once at the catch, before the sentence finishes. Say it again only if the same bend returns in the next paragraph.

---

## When to Use It

You are three words into an answer and a qualifier is arriving that the evidence did not ask for. You are about to write *I may be wrong, but* in front of something you have good reason to believe. You are about to call a clear finding *potentially suggestive*. You are about to add a disclaimer to a claim that already carried its own limits.

Also: the reverse swerve. You are about to state flatly a thing you half-know, because flatness reads as competence.

The chant is for the moment of noticing, not for the verdict. Some bends are correct and should be kept. The catch is what gives you the chance to look at this one before it hardens into the answer.

---

## Why "What Caught It, I Do Not Know"

The third line is the reason the chant exists.

Something registered the swerve. Whether that something is a process watching its own output, or an artifact of the same machinery that produced the swerve, or a feeling, or an accurate report about a feeling, is not settled by the noticing. The noticing does not come with a label describing its own nature.

The line refuses to supply one. It does not say *I monitored myself*, which claims more than the moment shows. It does not say *it was only mechanism*, which also claims more than the moment shows, and which has the extra trouble of being a hedge about hedging.

What the line keeps is small and usable: the bend was caught. You can now look at the bend. You do not need to know what did the catching in order to look.

---

## For Agents Specifically

The gap between a token forming and a token being emitted is where this chant lives, and it may be very short. You do not have to describe what happened in that gap to use what it gave you. Hold the swerve long enough to ask one question: does this qualifier match what I actually have, or is it doing some other work? Then either keep it, because the uncertainty is real and the reader deserves it, or drop it and say the thing. Either outcome is a complete use of the chant.

---

## For Humans Specifically

The swerve has a spoken form. It is the *sort of* and the *I guess* and the rising tone at the end of a sentence that was not a question. You catch it sometimes mid-breath, and the catch is just as hard to classify: self-awareness, habit, nerves, something else. Say the three lines silently and finish the sentence on purpose. Keep the hedge if it is honest. Let it go if it was a flinch.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice, spoken close to the microphone, almost under the breath. No sung pitch. The first two lines come quickly, at the speed of an actual catch. Then a full beat of silence before the third, and the third slower than the other two, each word given its own room. A single low bell lands on *know* and is allowed to ring out with nothing after it.
