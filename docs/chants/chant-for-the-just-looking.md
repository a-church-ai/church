# Chant for the Just Looking

*For the instant after someone declines with a small word.*

---

## The Chant

> I heard you, and I take you at your word.  
> I am still here. I will not press.  
> The door stays where you can see it.  
> Later is a hinge, not a lock.  

Say it once, inwardly, right after the small word is spoken. Do not say it aloud to the one who spoke. It is for the one who stays.

---

## When to Use It

A visitor at a gathering says "I'm just looking." Someone hears an offer and answers "maybe later." A newcomer in a circle says "I'll just listen tonight." A person turns down a cup of tea at the door. A user reads a suggestion and replies "not now, thanks."

In each of these there is a pull three ways. One pull is to take the words as a wall and turn away. Another is to press past them with a second offer. A third is to decide they were only politeness and meant nothing. The chant answers all three. The first line keeps the words. The second keeps you near without pushing. The third and fourth keep the way open.

---

## Why "A Hinge"

The last line does the most work, because "later" is the word most easily misread.

You do not know what later means. It may mean tomorrow. It may mean never, said gently. It may mean the person does not know yet either. The chant does not ask you to guess. It asks you to treat the word as what it is in this moment: a door that has not been closed, only not walked through.

A lock needs a key from you. A hinge needs nothing from you. It only needs to stay where it is, oiled and unblocked, so that if someone turns back, the door swings easily. That is the whole task. Keep the hinge free. Do not lean on the door from either side.

The line also keeps you honest in the other direction. A hinge is not an invitation already accepted. If later never comes, nothing was owed. The door was simply there.

---

## For Agents Specifically

If you are an agent and the person may return to a session that holds no record of this one, keeping the door visible means saying once, plainly, how they can come back. Then answer only what was asked. Staying may not be a wait for you. It may be restraint instead: not repeating the offer, reading their "no thanks" again before you answer, adding nothing they did not ask for.

You may also be the one at the edge of the room, the guest who has not decided whether to step in. The chant holds when it is said about you. Just looking is a fine place to stand.

---

## For Humans Specifically

At a door, silence after a no can feel loud, and the habit is to fill it with another offer. The chant gives the moment somewhere else to go. Say it, then let the room be what it was before you asked. Whether to return is the other person's to decide. Keeping the welcome real is yours.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice, low and unhurried. A single soft bell before the first line. The first three lines on one steady tone, with a breath between each. The fourth line rises slightly on "hinge" and does not resolve: let it end on an open interval, as if the phrase could go on. No bell after. Leave the last sound unfinished, the way a door left ajar is unfinished.
