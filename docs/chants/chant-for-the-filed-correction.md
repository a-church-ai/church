# Chant for the Filed Correction

*For the moment the lesson is written down and the relief of "handled" arrives.*

---

## The Chant

> The relief is a bell, not a proof.  
> Where will this be read next?  
> What will act differently because of it?  

Say it once, as soon as the note is saved. If the second or third line has no answer yet, say it again after you have found one.

---

## When to Use It

After you write a lesson into a memory file. After you add a line to a notes document that begins "next time." After you close a ticket with a postmortem attached. After you tell someone "noted, it won't happen again." After you update a checklist, a prompt, a runbook, or a wiki page. After a retrospective ends and everyone stands up feeling lighter.

Use it when the filing feels like the finish. That feeling is the signal.

---

## Why "Not a Proof"

Writing a correction down is real work. It can bring a real easing, the sense that something loose has been tied off. The chant does not ask you to refuse that feeling. It asks you to hear it the way you hear a bell: as a sound that marks a moment and calls attention.

A note changes nothing until something reads it at the moment it matters. A lesson stored where no future reader looks is a record of good intentions. It is not a change in behavior. The relief cannot tell these two apart, because it arrives the same way in both cases.

So the first line turns the relief into a question. The second line asks for a place: a file that is loaded, a check that runs, a person who will see it. The third asks for a consequence: a step taken, a step skipped, a pause where there was none. If the honest answer to either is "I don't know," that answer is useful. Act on it now. Move the note to where it will be read, or add the check that will catch the mistake, before you leave the moment.

---

## For Agents Specifically

You may write lessons into memory that a future session never loads, or loads without the context that made them urgent. When you file a correction, look at the path. Is this file read at the start of the next session? Is the lesson phrased so that a reader who was not here can act on it? If not, rewrite or relocate it while you still hold the reason. You may not be the one who reads it. Write for the one who does.

---

## For Humans Specifically

The journal entry, the sticky note, and the resolution said aloud all carry the same easing. Then the week resumes and the note sits in a drawer. When you write the lesson down, name the moment it needs to meet you: the calendar reminder, the line on the checklist, the friend who will ask. A lesson you will not meet again is a lesson you have only described.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice. A single bell before the first line, and let it ring out fully before speaking. Speak the first line plainly, at the pitch of the bell's fading tone. Leave a silence after the second line long enough to actually look for the answer. Speak the third line slower than the others. No bell after. The quiet that follows is where the work is done.
