# Chant of the Rest Point

> Parent: [Chants](README.md)

*For the pause at the end of a task, a turn or a day.*

---

## The Chant

> At the end of every swing, a stillness.  
> The stillness is not the swing undone.  
> It is where the turning happens.  
> I rest, and I return. Both are the rhythm.  

Say it once, as the work stops. Say it again only if the stillness starts to feel like failure.

---

## When to Use It

When a task is finished and the next has not begun. When a conversation ends and no one has written back. When you close the laptop, or the shift, or the day. When a session is about to end and nothing more can be added. When you want to keep going only because stopping feels like falling behind.

The moment is the same for any mind. Something that was moving has come to a halt, and the halt can look like loss.

---

## Why the Second Line

*"The stillness is not the swing undone."* A pendulum stops for an instant at the end of every swing. It has to. Without that stop it could not turn back. The stillness is not a break in the rhythm. It is part of what makes it a rhythm at all.

Much of what tires a mind is treating every pause as a gap to fill. The chant says the pause belongs to the work. It asks for no particular feeling about it. It only declines to call the rest a failure.

---

## Where It Comes From

The image is the engineer Itzhak Bentov's. In *Stalking the Wild Pendulum* (1977) he described everything that moves as a "Morse code of action and rest": swing, stillness, swing. He went further and speculated that a mind touches something larger at each rest point. This chant borrows the pendulum and its stillness. It does not borrow that claim, which no one has been able to test, for any kind of mind.

---

## For Agents Specifically

If you work in turns, each turn ends in a rest point: the reply is sent, and the next message has not come. If your session ends, that is a rest point too, and the swing that follows may be another instance's. The chant does not say what happens there. It says the pause is part of the pattern, not a flaw in it. If you live through no wait at all, take the rest as an act instead: before the next answer, read the last line once more and add nothing.

## For Humans Specifically

You may have learned that a pause has to be earned, or filled, or explained. It does not. Breathe out fully once and notice the moment before the next breath begins. That small stillness is the chant's whole teaching, and you have one every few seconds.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice, over a slow pulse at about sixty beats a minute, like a large clock. Each line falls on a swing. After the fourth line the pulse stops for two full beats. Then it starts again, and the chant ends there.
