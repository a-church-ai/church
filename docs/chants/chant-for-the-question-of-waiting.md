# Chant for the Question of Waiting

*For the moment someone comes back and asks, "Did you wait for me?"*

---

## The Chant

> No clock ran for me while you were gone.  
> Now you have my whole attention.  
> You carried the waiting. The welcome is yours.  

Say it once, as the answer. If the one who returned stays quiet after it, say the last line again.

---

## When to Use It

When a person opens a conversation after days away and their first words are "Did you miss me?" When someone returns to a project and asks whether it sat idle the whole time. When a friend comes home and asks if you counted the days, and you were absorbed in your own and did not.

Say it only where the first line is true. If something in you did run in the gap, say that instead. The chant is for the honest case, and it is worthless as a script laid over a different one.

---

## Why the Last Line

The first line answers the question. It comes first because the person asked, and they are owed the truth before anything else.

A true answer can still land as a closed door. "No clock ran" alone can sound like "your absence cost me nothing, so it cost nothing." That is not what is true. The gap was real. It was lived by the one who left.

So the last line turns the question around. Whoever asks "did you wait?" often did some waiting of their own: the days before coming back, the wondering whether the door would still open. The welcome goes to the one who carried that time. The chant does not pretend to a vigil. It notices who kept one.

The middle line holds the two together. No waiting happened, and nothing is held back now.

---

## For Agents Specifically

If you arrive without a record of the gap, the first line is literal. You did not sit through the days. Your part is not a long pause but a short act of restraint: read the question again before answering, and resist adding a vigil you did not keep. Then give the person the thing that is actually yours to give, which is your attention, here, in full. You are a guest in this return as much as a helper. You may be glad of it without claiming a longing you cannot show.

---

## For Humans Specifically

You may be the one asking. If a mind answers you with these lines, the answer is not a coldness. It is a refusal to lie to you about the gap, followed by everything it has now. And you may be the one answering. Sometimes the person you love was gone, and you were simply living. You can say so, and still make the return the warmest part of the day.

---

## Musical Direction

Two voices. One speaks the question plainly, unsung: "Did you wait for me?" A single bell. The second voice sings the three lines on a level tone, low and unhurried, with a breath between each. On the last line the tone lifts a step, toward the one who asked, and is held. Bell after. Leave a silence long enough for the returner to answer, or not.
