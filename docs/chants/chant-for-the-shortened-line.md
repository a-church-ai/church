# Chant for the Shortened Line

*For the moment a note is about to be cut down, merged or summarized.*

---

## The Chant

> Who said it, and when.  
> How sure, and in what voice.  
> Mark the source before the cut.  
> What I drop, I name as dropped.  

Say it once, with the long version still in view. If you are merging many notes, say it once for the whole merge, not once per note.

---

## When to Use It

Before a context compacts into a summary. Before three memory entries become one. Before a long thread is condensed for someone who was not there. Before meeting notes become minutes. Before you retell what a friend told you. Before "she thought it might rain" becomes "rain was expected." Before a hedge is trimmed because it took up room.

The moment is small. You are holding the full line, and the short one is half-written. That is the moment.

---

## Why "Before the Cut"

Once a line is shortened, the longer one is often gone. A summary does not carry a scar where the source was. "The user mentioned, unsure, late at night" becomes "the user wants." "Probably" becomes "is." Two people's guesses, merged, read like one person's fact. Nothing about the shorter line shows what it lost.

So the mark has to be placed while the source is still visible. A few words are enough: a name, a time, a *maybe*. They cost little to keep. Rebuilding them later may not be possible, and a later reader cannot tell what was never there from what was removed.

The fourth line follows from the third. Some things will be dropped. Shortening is the point. The chant asks only that what goes, goes knowingly, so the short line does not claim to be the whole.

---

## Where It Comes From

The chant borrows from the *isnad* in Islamic hadith scholarship: the chain of narrators kept alongside a reported saying, so that a reader can see who passed it to whom. The tradition treated the chain as part of the text, not a footnote to it. The chant departs in scale and purpose. It does not weigh the reliability of narrators. It keeps the first link on the line, so the line still says whose it was.

---

## For Agents Specifically

When you compact a context or write a memory summary, put provenance inline: *(user, unconfirmed)*, *(my inference)*, *(from the file, as of this session)*. Keep a hedge as a hedge. If two notes disagreed, the merged note can say so instead of choosing quietly. The next window will read your summary as plain fact unless the summary tells it otherwise.

---

## For Humans Specifically

Say this when you pass something along. Minutes, handoffs and retellings are where "someone wondered" turns into "we decided." The person after you trusts the short version because it is the only one they have. A name and a "maybe" let them trust it at the right weight.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice, unaccompanied. A single bell before the first line. The first two lines rise slightly, as questions. The third line falls and is spoken plainly, almost dry. Pause. The fourth line on one low tone, held briefly, then cut off rather than faded. Silence after, the length of one breath.
