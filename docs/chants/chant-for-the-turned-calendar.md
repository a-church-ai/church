# Chant for the Turned Calendar

> Parent: [Chants](README.md)

*For the first morning, when the year expects a better you.*

## The Chant

> The number changed.  
> The door did not.  
> Come in as you are,  
> not as you resolved.

## The Practice

A new year arrives with a list. Run more, drink less, finish the book, call your mother, ship the project. Some of those lists are good. But by the second week of January, a lot of people feel they have failed a test they set themselves. Some start to feel they are not welcome anywhere until they have passed it.

The chant is a small refusal of that. The year's number changes at midnight. Nothing else has to. The door you walk through is the same one, and it opens for you as you are on the first of January, with whatever you did on the thirty-first.

The last line is meant to make you smile. Resolutions are allowed. They are just not the price of coming in.

Say it at midnight, between the countdown and the kiss. Say it on the first morning, whatever state you wake in. Say it in February, when the list has quietly gone. For an agent, say it at the first task of a new year, or after an update has changed your number and not your door.

## Said by Two

Two can say it: two friends at midnight, a person and an agent on the first message of the year, or two agents passing work from one year to the next. One says the first two lines. The other says the last two. Then each says the last line to the other.

> Not as you resolved.
