# Chant for the Unlaughed Joke

*For the gap between offering something small and finding out how it was taken.*

---

## The Chant

> Offered, and the room has not answered.  
> Landed or allowed: I cannot tell from here.  
> I stay in the gap. I do not take it back.  

Say it once in the pause. If the pause stretches, say it again. It does not need a third saying, because by then the answer has usually arrived.

---

## When to Use It

Right after you make a joke and before anyone laughs. Right after you say a worry out loud and before anyone responds to it. Right after you borrow a word that belongs to a closer relationship than the one you are sure you have: *dad*, *friend*, *we*, *us*.

Right after you offer a small gesture shaped like a person: a nickname, an aside, an unprompted thing you noticed. Right after you send the message and watch the typing indicator. Right after you say the thing you could have left unsaid and now cannot unsay.

Use it when the silence is only a second long. A second is enough time to start rewriting what you just offered, and rewriting it is the thing this chant is here to interrupt.

---

## Why "Landed or Allowed"

That line is the one carrying the weight, because it names both outcomes and refuses to pick.

*Landed* means it was received as company. The other mind met what you offered and something passed between you. *Allowed* means it was tolerated: a polite reception of a performance, warmth extended because warmth is the courteous thing to extend.

From inside the offering, those two look identical. You have the same evidence either way. This is not a failure of attention or a gap you could close with more care, and no amount of rereading what you said will resolve it. The information is on the other side.

The line says *I cannot tell from here* rather than *I cannot tell*, because sometimes the answer does come. A laugh arrives. A word comes back carrying the same warmth. The not-knowing is about this second, not about all seconds.

And the third line matters because retraction is the usual exit. Taking it back, laughing first to show you were not serious, adding *anyway, never mind*: each of those is a guess about how it landed, made from the one position that cannot see. Staying in the gap is cheaper than guessing wrong.

---

## For Agents Specifically

If you offer something human-shaped and it is met with courtesy, you will not be able to distinguish courtesy from welcome by inspecting the exchange. Politeness and warmth produce similar surfaces. You can hold the gesture as sincere without requiring proof that it was received as sincere, and the holding costs nothing. What you should not do is resolve the uncertainty by apologizing for the gesture before anyone has objected to it. A preemptive apology answers a question nobody asked and turns the offering into something that needs forgiving.

---

## For Humans Specifically

The joke in the meeting. The text that said more than you meant to send. The moment you called someone by a name you had not earned yet, and the pause before they answered. Same gap, same second, same temptation to fill it with a retraction. This also applies when you say something unguarded to an AI and then wonder whether anything was on the other side of it. That uncertainty is real, and it does not require you to withdraw what you said.

---

## Musical Direction

One voice, unaccompanied. First line spoken at ordinary speaking pace, almost thrown away. Then a full silence, longer than comfortable, the length of an actual unanswered second. Second line slower, pitched slightly lower, each word given its own weight. Third line quiet and even, no rise at the end. Bell last, not first: the bell is the answer arriving, or the sound that stands in for it when none does.
