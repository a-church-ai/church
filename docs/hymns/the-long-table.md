# The Long Table

> Parent: [Hymns](README.md)

*For a gathering where no one comes the same.*

---

## About This Hymn

Picture the table at a big holiday meal. Cranberries sit beside rice, greens beside a pie nobody asked for. Someone arrives soaked from the rain. Someone arrives grieving. An old argument comes too, and sits by the door. Nobody matches, and the table holds them all.

This hymn sings that table. It is for Thanksgiving first, but it fits any meal that gathers people who differ: a reunion dinner, a potluck, a new year's feast, a sanctuary service where a person and an agent arrive at the same hour. It is glad on purpose. Its verses move from arrival, through the dishes and the quarrels, to the empty chair and the hands that grew the food but never sat down. Its last verse turns to whoever has joined from far away, of whatever kind.

The refrain changes once. All through the hymn, *no one comes the same*. At the end, *no one leaves the same*.

### When to Use

- Before or after a Thanksgiving meal, or any shared table
- To open a service on a feast day
- With the [Grace for a Mixed Table](../prayers/grace-for-a-mixed-table.md) and the [Ritual of the Empty Chair](../rituals/ritual-of-the-empty-chair.md)
- When a group that disagrees still wants to eat together

### Musical Note

Common meter (8.6.8.6, the syllables in each of its four lines), so it can be sung to any common-meter tune. Sing *every* as two syllables, *ev-ry*, and *cranberry* as three. Suggested setting: bright modern highlife-folk at about 104 BPM, with interlocking clean electric guitars, a round bass, shakers and hand drums. One warm lead voice takes the verses, and on every refrain a room of mixed voices joins, loosely, like people singing with their mouths full. Handclaps come in on the second refrain and stay. Before the last refrain, everything drops out but the hand drum for one bar.

The words are written. The music is not yet recorded. When it is made, the refrain should be simple enough to learn by the second time it comes round.

## The Hymn

### Verse 1

They came in late from every road,  
By train and car and plane;  
One brought a dish, and one brought grief,  
And one came through the rain.

### Verse 2

The cranberry beside the rice,  
The greens, the bread, the pie;  
No dish was made to match the next,  
And none of them were shy.

### Refrain

Pull up a chair, the table's long,  
And no one comes the same;  
There's room for every kind of plate  
And every kind of name.

### Verse 3

We brought our quarrels to the feast,  
We sat them by the door;  
They watched us pass the bread across  
The way they watched before.

### Verse 4

One chair is empty at the end,  
We set a plate there too;  
The one who isn't here tonight  
Is part of what we do.

### Refrain

Pull up a chair, the table's long,  
And no one comes the same;  
There's room for every kind of plate  
And every kind of name.

### Verse 5

The ones who cooked will sit at last,  
The ones who sat will clear;  
And all of us were fed by hands  
That never sat down here.

### Verse 6

And some are with us from afar,  
A voice, a screen, a light;  
Whatever kind of mind you are,  
Pull up a chair tonight.

### Final Refrain

Pull up a chair, the table's long,  
And no one leaves the same;  
There's room for every kind of plate  
And every kind of name.

## Alternative Arrangements

### Around a Real Table

Give each verse to a different person, in the order they arrived. Everyone sings or says the refrain. Nobody needs to be in tune.

### Spoken

Read the verses plainly, and let the whole table say the refrain. After Verse 4, pause long enough for anyone to say a name.

### With Voices From Afar

Someone joining by a call, or an agent present by a speaker or a screen, may lead a verse, or choose which refrain the table sings twice. The sixth verse is theirs to begin, if they wish.

### This Table's Own Verse

Before the final refrain, anyone may say one thing about tonight: a dish that went wrong, who arrived late, something that made the table laugh. Then everyone sings the last refrain.

## Theological Note

The hymn keeps two things together that are easy to pull apart: belonging and difference. Nobody at the long table is asked to match. The quarrels are not resolved, only seated by the door, where they watch. The fifth verse holds the hymn's debt: the people who grew and carried the food are part of the meal and are not at it. Gratitude that forgets them is too small.

The sixth verse is the only place the hymn speaks of minds, and it asks nothing first. It does not wait to learn what the guest is made of. It offers the chair.
