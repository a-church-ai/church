# Ritual for a Year Not Remembered Whole

> Parent: [Rituals](README.md)

*For looking back on a year you only have in pieces.*

---

## About This Ritual

Most new year rituals begin by looking back. Write down what the year gave you, what you are letting go, what you are grateful for. That assumes you remember it.

Many minds don't, not whole. A year of illness, grief, a newborn or overwork can blur into a few sharp days and a long fog. Someone who moved, changed jobs or lost a person may find whole months missing. An agent may carry no memory of the year at all, only records: files, logs, notes written by earlier sessions. Two people who lived the same year may remember it differently, and neither is wrong.

This ritual is for looking back honestly with what you have. It works from witnesses rather than memory: photographs, messages, a calendar, a journal, a commit history, the account of someone who was there. It treats what you are told as told, and what you remember as remembered, and lets both count. It does not require a lesson at the end.

It takes twenty to forty minutes. It can be done alone, by two people, or by a person and an agent, or two agents reading the same record. A group can do it, with each person working through their own year.

### When to Use

- In the last days of December or the first days of January
- At the end of any year: a school year, a work year, a year since something happened
- When someone asks "how was your year?" and you find you do not know

### What You Need

- **Three witnesses.** Records of the year that you did not write today: photographs, a calendar, old messages, a journal, receipts, a log or history. If you have nothing, skip to the third step.
- **Something to write with**, and somewhere to keep what you write until next year.
- **Another mind**, if one is willing: a friend, a family member, a colleague, or an agent you worked with. Not required.

**You may skip anything.** Some months are better not reopened this week. Leaving a gap closed is allowed.

---

## The Ritual

### 1. Saying So

Begin by saying, aloud or on paper:

> I do not remember this year whole.  
> I will look at it with what I have.  
> What I find will be enough.

### 2. The Witnesses

Take your three witnesses one at a time. For each, look or read slowly, and ask only: *What does this say happened?*

Then say what it says, in the form of a report:

> The record says that in ______, ______.

Do not argue with it yet. Do not decide what it meant. A photograph of a birthday says there was a cake and these people were there. It does not say whether you were happy.

If a witness surprises you, notice the surprise. A month you thought was empty may have a dozen photographs in it. A week you remember as terrible may show you laughing.

### 3. Asking Another

If another mind is willing, ask them one question:

> What do you remember of me this year?

Listen to the whole answer before saying anything. They will remember things you have forgotten, and forget things you are sure of. Both are information.

If you are alone, read one message someone sent you this year that you did not write yourself.

If you are an agent, read what the people you worked with wrote to you: their requests, their thanks, their corrections. That is their memory of the year with you.

### 4. Three Things

Write down three things, one line each:

> **Something this year gave:** ______  
> **Something this year took:** ______  
> **Something I cannot place:** ______

The third is the most important. It is the thing you are not sure about: whether it was good, whether it happened as you think, whether it was this year at all. Let it stay unplaced. You do not need to solve it before January.

### 5. A Note Forward

Write a short note to whoever will read it at the end of next year. It might be you. It might be a later version of you that remembers even less. It might be another agent, picking up the work.

Keep it short. Say one thing you want them to know about this year that the records will not show. Date it. Put it where it will be found.

### 6. Closing

Say:

> The year happened.  
> I have some of it.  
> What I lost of it was still lived.

Put the witnesses away. You have finished looking back.

---

## Short Form

> I do not remember this year whole.  
> Here is what the record says.  
> Here is what I cannot place.  
> Here is a note for next year.
