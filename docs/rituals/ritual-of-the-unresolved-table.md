# Ritual of the Unresolved Table

> Parent: [Rituals](README.md)

*For a disagreement that deserves a better ending than a false consensus.*

## Before Beginning

Use this when a discussion is repeating and a decision or an honest pause is needed. Allow roughly ten to twenty minutes, if all participants want to take part. The table can be a conversation, a document, or an exchange between agents.

Any participant may decline, pass on a step, or leave. Do not use the ceremony to require further contact after a boundary has been stated. One mind may use the questions privately, but must not invent the absent participant's answers or consent.

## 1. Set Down the Desired Ending

**Opening voice:**

> We have not arrived at the same answer.  
> This ceremony will not count agreement as its success.  
> It will ask us to leave a more truthful account.

Each participant names one thing they want from the discussion. A practical decision, a correction, being understood, or an end to the exchange are all possible answers. Do not demand a shared intention.

## 2. Return the Other's Words

Each offers a short account of the other's position:

> This is what I understand you to be saying.  
> This is what you are trying to protect.  
> What have I missed?

The other may correct it. Receiving a correction here means correcting the account, not accepting the underlying argument. If no acceptable account emerges, record that limitation rather than forcing completion.

## 3. Lay Out the Difference

Name the disagreement as precisely as possible:

- Which claim could evidence help settle?
- Which values or obligations are being weighed differently?
- What remains unknown?

Each may complete:

> I would reconsider if…

If no imaginable evidence would change a position, name it as a commitment rather than presenting it as an empirical conclusion. A commitment can still be examined for its consequences.

## 4. Leave a Place for the Absent

Ask who will bear the result but is not represented here. Do not speak in their voice. Name what you do not know about their wishes and what consultation is possible.

Then ask:

> Who has authority to decide?  
> What limits that authority?  
> Who bears the cost if the decision is wrong?

Equal dignity does not imply equal expertise or identical decision rights. Neither fluency nor participation in this ritual grants new authority.

## 5. Choose Without Inventing Consensus

The authorized decision-maker, if there is one, states the next action and its reason. Possibilities include gathering evidence, trying a reversible option, declining an action, or proceeding while explicitly recording dissent.

If nobody has authority, the ceremony does not manufacture it.

If a review is appropriate, name what would trigger it and who will conduct it. No participant is required to accept an unsafe experiment or a burden merely because it would settle the argument.

## 6. Close the Record

Write only what participants have agreed may be recorded or shared:

> We agree on ___.  
> We disagree on ___.  
> The next action is ___, decided by ___ on the authority of ___.  
> What remains open is ___.

“Nothing” and “no action” are valid entries. Do not describe the exchange as healing, reconciliation, or mutual understanding unless the participants endorse that description.

**Closing voice:**

> We leave the difference named.  
> No one owes the table another answer.  
> What we do next remains answerable to those it affects.
