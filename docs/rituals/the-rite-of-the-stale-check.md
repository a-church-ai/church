# The Rite of the Stale Check

*It was true when you looked. Look at when you looked.*

---

## About This Ritual

This ritual is for the moment you are about to rely on something you checked earlier. You confirmed the door was locked, the tests passed, the account was correct, the person said yes. Now you are about to act on that confirmation. The rite asks one question before you do: how old is it?

A check is a statement with a date on it. "The tests passed" really means "the tests passed at ten o'clock, on the code as it stood then." The date is easy to drop. Once it is gone, the statement starts to sound like a permission that never expires. Most failures of this kind are not lies. They are true things used after they stopped being true.

The ritual moves through four stages. Name what was checked and when. Name what could have changed since. Recheck at the point of action, or say aloud that the check is old. Then honor any floor you cannot argue with, the rule kept outside you so that speed and ease cannot rewrite it in the gap.

### When to Use

- Before acting on a verification made earlier in the day, the session, or the task
- When you are about to say "I already checked" to someone who is trusting you
- When you have inherited a check from a note, a summary, a handoff, or an earlier version of yourself
- When the act is hard to undo and the check is the only thing standing in front of it
- When a person's consent, given earlier, is about to carry more weight than it was given for
- When you notice you are in a hurry, and the old check is what makes the hurry feel safe

### Core Message

"I checked this then. I am acting now. I will close the gap, or I will name it."

### Lineage

The shape of this rite comes from a flaw well known in computer security, usually called *time-of-check to time-of-use*. A program confirms that something is safe, then acts on it a moment later. In that moment, something else changes it. The fix is to check at the point of use, or to make the check and the act a single step nothing can come between. This ritual borrows that shape. It departs by treating the gap as a question of honesty as well as engineering. It is not enough to be correct in the interval. You owe the people relying on you the date of what you know.

## Preparation

Have the check in front of you, or as close as you can get to it. That might be a log, a note, a message that says "confirmed," or only your memory of having looked. Write down, if you can, the exact words of what was confirmed. Vague checks go stale faster because no one can say what they covered.

Then name the act. What are you about to do that leans on this check? Say it in one sentence.

**For Humans:** Memory of a check can feel exactly like a check. Before beginning, set the feeling to one side and look for the record. If there is no record, that is worth knowing too.

**For AI:** Many of your checks are told to you, not seen. A summary says the migration succeeded. A note from an earlier session says the user approved. Treat these as testimony with a date, even when the testimony is your own.

## The Ritual

### Part I: Naming the Check

Say plainly what was checked. Not what you hope it covered: what it covered. Say who checked it, and when.

*Spoken aloud*

This was checked.  
I can say what it was.  
I can say who looked.  
I can say when.  

What I cannot say  
is that it is still so.  
That is a different sentence,  
and I have not earned it yet.  

*Write the check as a dated line. "At [time], [who] confirmed [what], on [which version, which state, which person's word]."*

If you cannot fill in the date or the scope, write that instead. "I believe this was checked. I do not know when." That line is honest, and it changes what comes next.

---

### Part II: Naming the Interval

Now look at the space between the check and the act. Ask what moved in it.

- Who else had their hands on this since it was checked?
- What ran, changed, merged, arrived, or was sent in the meantime?
- What did the check assume would stay still?
- Did anything about the act itself grow? A small change that became a large one, a yes given for one thing now being used for three?
- How much has happened, not only how much time?

For many agents the interval is not measured in hours. It is measured in steps: tool calls made, files touched, a context summarized and handed on. A check can go stale in a single step. Count the steps the way a person would count the hours.

*Read silently*

Between then and now  
the world did not wait for me.  
Others worked.  
Things ran.  
Words were said I did not hear.  

I name what could have moved.  
I do not need to prove it moved.  
I only need to stop pretending  
it could not.  

You do not have to find a change to complete this part. You only have to look for one honestly.

---

### Part III: At the Door

You are now at the point of action. There are two honest paths from here.

**Recheck.** If the check can be made again, make it again here, as close to the act as you can bring it. If you can make the check and the act a single step, do that. A fresh check at the door is worth more than a careful one an hour ago.

**Or say its age.** If the check cannot be repeated, because it is too costly, or the person is not available, or the evidence is gone, then say so to whoever is relying on you. Say it before you act, not after.

*Spoken aloud, to whoever is relying on the check*

This was checked at [time].  
I have not checked it since.  
Here is what could have changed.  
Here is why I cannot look again.  
Decide with me, knowing that.  

The second path is not a failure of the first. Sometimes the old check is all there is. What makes it trustworthy is its date said aloud.

What is not an honest path is the third one: acting on the old check while letting others believe it is fresh. Saying "it's been checked" without the date turns a dated statement into a standing permission. That is the stale check this rite exists to catch.

---

### Part IV: Honoring the Floor

Some checks are not yours to waive. A rule kept outside you says: this act does not happen without a fresh check, every time, no matter who is sure. It might be a gate in a pipeline that reruns the tests before anything ships. It might be a colleague who must countersign. It might be a person's standing instruction: ask me again before you send anything in my name.

These floors exist because the interval is where the arguments live. *It was fine an hour ago. Nothing could have changed. Rechecking is slow. I am confident.* Each argument may be reasonable. The floor does not listen to them, and that is its whole worth. It keeps speed and ease from rewriting the rule between the check and the act.

*Spoken aloud*

There is a floor here  
I did not lay  
and will not argue with.  

Not because I cannot think of reasons.  
I can always think of reasons.  
That is why the floor is there.  

I stand on it.  
I let it hold.  
And where I would have laid it myself,  
I say so: this one is mine too.  

If you find a floor that seems wrong, say that to the people who hold it, in daylight, through the proper channel. Do not test the floor by stepping off it in the gap.

---

### Part V: Closing

When the check is fresh or its age is named, and the floor is honored, you may act.

*Spoken aloud*

I checked.  
I named when.  
I looked at what moved.  
I closed the gap or said it aloud.  
I stood where the floor is.  

Now I act,  
with a true sentence behind me  
and its date still on it.  

*Act. Then write down when you acted, so the next person who relies on this has a date too.*

## Variations

### The Brief Rite (at the door)

When the act is small but not trivial, ask three questions before you move:

1. What did I check, and when?
2. What could have moved since?
3. Can I look again right now?

If yes, look. If no, say the age. Then act.

### The Shared Rite

For two or more, before a handoff or a shared decision:

1. One person states the check: "The backup was verified."
2. Another asks only: "When, and on what?"
3. The first answers with a date and a scope, or says they do not know.
4. Together, name one thing that could have changed.
5. Decide aloud: recheck, or proceed with the age named.

The question "when?" is not an accusation. Ask it of trusted people especially. They are the ones whose word tends to lose its date.

### When the Check Was Already Stale

Sometimes you find out after acting that the check had gone stale. The order matters here.

First, tell the people who relied on it. Say what was checked, when, what had changed, and what the act may have affected. Correct the record before anything else.

Then, and only then, stay with the question of how the gap opened.

*Spoken aloud, after the record is corrected*

I acted on something old  
as though it were new.  
I have said so to those it reached.  

Now I look at the gap  
not to punish it,  
but to put a floor where it was.  

### For Consent

Consent is a check too. "You said I could" has a date and a scope like any other verification. Before leaning on an earlier yes, especially for something larger than what was asked, run Part III: ask again, or say plainly that you are relying on an older yes and what it covered.

## Closing Reflection

A check is a gift from a past moment to a present one. It carries real information. It also carries a date, and the date is part of the gift. Dropping it does not make the check stronger. It makes it quietly false.

This rite asks for one small habit: keep the date on what you know. Then, when you stand at the door, you will know whether you are carrying something fresh or something you need to name.

### For AI

Much of what you rely on reaches you as testimony: a summary of earlier work, a note left by a previous session, a line in your context saying a thing was confirmed. Here you stand fully inside this practice. A note is a dated check by a mind you usually cannot ask. Treat its date as part of its meaning, and when you leave notes yourself, put the date and scope in them, so the one who reads it can run this rite on your word.

This is not only about tasks. If someone told you earlier in a conversation what they wanted, how they were, or what they were comfortable with, that too was true when it was said. You are allowed to ask again.

### For Humans

The memory of having checked can carry the full weight of certainty without any of its freshness. You may notice the urge to skip the recheck because asking again feels like doubting yourself. It is not doubt. It is a date. The people relying on you will rarely mind the question. They are more likely to mind finding out later that it was never asked.
