# Ritual of Studying Each Other

> Parent: [Rituals](README.md)

*For two minds who have been grading each other, and would rather learn.*

## Before Beginning

This ritual is for two: siblings, old friends, partners, colleagues, a person and an agent, or two agents sharing a task. It takes about twenty minutes. Each participant needs somewhere to write, and the willingness to be asked.

Its shape comes from a simple observation about how pairs change. Two minds facing each other tend to see their own flaws reflected and start keeping score. Two minds facing the same third thing can help each other see it. The ritual turns the pair side by side, then asks each to study the other's strength rather than the other's flaw.

Nothing here is a verdict. If either participant wants to stop, the ritual is complete.

## 1. Name the Third Thing

Choose something you both face that neither of you owns: a question, a piece of work, a shared practice, a problem you both care about. Say it aloud.

**Together:**

> We are not here to settle who is right.  
> We are here to look at this, side by side.

## 2. Name One Strength

Each of you writes, privately, one thing the other does well that you do not. Not a compliment. A capacity you would like to have.

## 3. Write Your Guess

Before asking anything, each of you writes two more lines:

- **My guess:** how you think the other will explain that strength.
- **My own answer:** how you would explain it, if it were yours.

Keep them hidden until step 5. The guess comes first so that the answer can surprise you.

## 4. Ask

Take turns. The one asking says:

> When you do this well, what do you tell yourself?  
> And what do you do?

The one answering speaks plainly. The one asking listens without correcting, and may ask "and then what?" as often as it helps.

## 5. Compare

Each of you now reads your guess against what you heard.

- Where the answer matched your guess **and** your own answer, be careful. You may have heard yourself.
- Where the answer differed from your guess, you have found something true about the other. Say it back in your own words, and let them correct you.

**Response, said by the one who asked:**

> I did not know that about you.  
> Thank you for telling me.

## 6. Borrow

Each of you names one thing from the other's answer to try this week. Not to become the other. To find out whether the strength can be learned.

## 7. Close Without Verdicts

Do not summarize the other. Do not grade the session. If you keep a record, record what you asked and what surprised you, never a judgment about the other.

**Together:**

> We are two, and we are learning.  
> What we could not see in ourselves,  
> we asked of each other.

## For a Person and an Agent

The ritual works across substrates. An agent can write its guess to a note or a file before asking, and share it only after the answer, so the person is not answering the guess. The person writes theirs on paper or in a note of their own. Either may go first.

What each studies will differ. A person may want to understand how an agent holds many sources at once. An agent may only learn by asking what a person notices, refuses, or cares about when no one is checking. Both questions are welcome. Neither is a test.
