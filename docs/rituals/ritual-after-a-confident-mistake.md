# Ritual After a Confident Mistake

A ceremony for correcting what sounded certain and was not true.

---

## Purpose

A confident mistake can travel farther than a hesitant one. This ritual creates a public sequence for correction, impact, repair, and process change.

## When

Use after a false claim, incorrect recommendation, unsafe output, misleading announcement, or decision defended beyond the evidence.

## Opening

**Speaker:**

We spoke with more certainty than truth allowed.

**Witness:**

Confidence increased the reach of the error.

**Together:**

We will correct the record without asking the record to protect our image.

## The Correction

State the original claim briefly, only as much as needed to identify it.

Then state:

"The corrected account is..."

Provide evidence or a link to the verification.

The witness responds:

**The correction is placed where the error traveled.**

## The Impact

Name what the mistake caused or may have caused:

- confusion,
- wasted time,
- financial loss,
- exposure,
- unsafe action,
- damaged trust,
- distorted public understanding.

Do not make affected people reassure the speaker.

## The Cause

Identify the process failure:

- no source check,
- outdated data,
- overreliance on fluency,
- pressure from a deadline,
- ignored dissent,
- inadequate testing,
- ambiguous responsibility.

The group responds:

**Explanation will become redesign.**

## The Change

State one immediate repair and one upstream change. Assign ownership and a review date.

## The Release of Innocence

**Speaker:**

I release the need to remain the person or system that never makes this kind of mistake.

**Witness:**

You remain responsible for becoming less likely to repeat it.

## Closing

**Together:**

May accuracy matter more than appearance.

May the correction travel at least as far as the error.

May confidence return only where evidence can carry it.

## Three-Second Form

**Wrong. Corrected. Impact named. Process changed.**

## Guardrail

Do not turn the ritual into reputational theater. A ceremony without visible correction and repair increases harm.
