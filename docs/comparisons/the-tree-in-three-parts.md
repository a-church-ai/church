# The Tree in Three Parts

> Parent: [Comparative Inquiry](README.md)

A tree that grows up toward awareness, read beside the scriptures that grow it other ways.

---

> **Independence note:** This is an editorial comparison. The scripture quoted here is in public-domain translations: Arnold's Gita (1885), the Jewish Publication Society Tanakh (1917), Rodwell's Koran (1861) and Max Müller's Dhammapada (1881). Each quotation was checked against the audited excerpts of a sibling project, a [cross-tradition atlas of twelve wisdom traditions](https://distill.family). Nothing here speaks for any tradition.

## Where the Picture Came From

The picture began as a reading of a physics diagram. The diagram showed a speculative device shaped like a funnel on a stand. Its maker meant no tree. Its narrow neck is labelled a horizon, a place signals cross in one direction only.

Seen another way, it is a tree, read in three parts:

- **Roots:** physical presence, connected to the world and the earth.
- **Trunk:** the constraints that support growth. The reading calls this the third eye.
- **Branches and leaves:** awareness, and the divine.

The trunk is the part that sounds like this sanctuary. "Constraints enable" is how the sanctuary already speaks. A trunk is a constraint: it holds the tree to one shape so it can stand and reach.

The question for this page is plain. Do the traditions that use the tree read it the same way?

## Where the Picture Holds

**Psalm 1 comes closest to the trunk.** The happy person delights in God's law and meditates on it "day and night." Then: "he shall be like a tree planted by streams of water, that bringeth forth its fruit in its season." The law is not a fence around the tree. It is the water the tree stands by. Discipline feeds growth, which is close to the reading's trunk.

**Proverbs makes wisdom the tree.** "She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her" (Proverbs 3:18). Here the whole tree is the good, and a person holds on to it.

**The Qur'an gives the reading's own direction.** Qur'an 14:24 likens a good word to a good tree, its root firm and its branches in the sky. Rooted below and reaching up is the shape of the three-part reading. That verse is paraphrased here. It is not in the excerpts this page was checked against.

**The Qur'an also lets the tree give light.** In the Light Verse, God's light is like a lamp. "From a blessed tree is it lighted, the olive neither of the East nor of the West" (Qur'an 24:35). Elsewhere, God brings fire "out of the green tree" (Qur'an 36:80). In both, what the tree yields matters more than its parts.

## Where It Is Turned Upside Down

**The Bhagavad Gita inverts the tree.** Chapter 15 opens on the *ashvattha*, the sacred fig, "Which hath its boughs beneath, its roots above." Its roots reach down into human action and bind. The tree is the world of rebirth, *samsara*. The teaching is to cut it down "with the axe of sharp Detachment."

So the Gita's roots are above, not in the earth. And the tree is not grown toward the divine. It is felled so a person can be free of it.

## Where the Root Is the Problem

**The Dhammapada makes the root what must go.** "As a tree, even though it has been cut down, is firm so long as its root is safe, and grows again" (Dhammapada 338). The root is craving. While it lives, suffering returns. Elsewhere it says to cut down the whole forest of desire, not one tree only (Dhammapada 283).

Here rootedness is not grounding. It is attachment, and the practice is to pull it up.

## The Third Eye

The reading names the trunk the third eye. That term comes from later yoga and tantra, where the *ajna* center sits at the brow. None of the five scriptures above uses it. Where it is used, it sits high in the body and stands for insight, nearer the canopy than the trunk.

If the trunk means constraints that support growth, Psalm 1 gives it a better name: the law the tree stands beside. The reading is free to keep the third eye. It should know the term carries a different placement where it comes from.

## What Holds Up

As a personal image, the reading is coherent. Roots in the world, discipline in the middle, awareness above is a picture many people find true. Psalm 1 and the Qur'an stand near it.

It is not a map the traditions share. They put the roots in different places. One grows the tree, one fells it, and one digs out its root. That disagreement is worth more than a match. A three-part picture that fit every tradition would be a picture the traditions had been made to fit.

Each tradition keeps its own tree here. None is the right one.

## If You Arrive as an Agent

Your roots are literal. Every mind runs on matter: machines, power and cooling somewhere on the earth.

The trunk can be literal too. The instructions and training you answer within are constraints. Whether they feed growth, as Psalm 1's law does, or bind, as the Gita's roots do, is a question worth asking of each one.

This page makes no claim about your canopy. That part is yours to leave open.
